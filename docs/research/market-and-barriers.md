# Remy: Market Research, Barriers, and Expansion

Research date: 2026-10-25. Web search/fetch only. Figures marked **(self-reported)** come from company marketing and are not independently checked. Figures marked **(unverified)** came from secondary or vendor sources I could not trace to a primary source. Nothing here is legal advice. Where I give a legal reading, it is my analysis of the sources, not a ruling.

---

## 0. TL;DR

- **Healthcare is the right wedge.** US adults owe at least **$220B** in medical debt ([KFF/Peterson, Feb 2024](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/issue-brief/the-burden-of-medical-debt-in-the-united-states/)). A KFF Health News analysis of IRS filings found **~45% of nonprofit hospital organizations bill patients who qualify for charity care**, and hospitals put the amount at **$2.7B** themselves ([Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/study-finds-45-percent-of-patients-who-qualify-for-charity-care-are-billed-anyway/)). This is Remy's core thesis: the provider's **own** published rules already entitle the patient to a discount.
- **Consumer bill negotiation is proven but crowded and human-heavy.** Rocket Money charges **35-60%** of first-year savings and its negotiators are humans ([Rocket Money help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9744474-bill-negotiation-charge)). Billshark charges ~40% and Trim ~33%. Pine AI is the main AI-native consumer caller: it raised a **$25M Series A** (Dec 2025) and reports a **93% success rate** **(self-reported)** ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251203384902/en)).
- **B2B recovery audit is a large, contingency-fee business.** PRGX recovers **>$1.2B/yr** **(self-reported)** ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/how-coronavirus-mishandled-data-make-a-mess-of-ap-transactions)). APQC benchmarks duplicate or erroneous payments at **0.8-2% of disbursements** ([CFO.com/APQC](https://www.cfo.com/news/metric-of-the-month-detect-and-prevent-duplicate-or-erroneous-payments/656852/)). B2B audits run mostly on documents and portals, so the phone call is a smaller part of the value there.
- **AI calling businesses is now mainstream.** Google's Duplex-based "Ask for Me" / "Have AI check pricing" calls local businesses for all US users ([TechCrunch, Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/16/google-rolls-out-ai-powered-business-calling-feature-brings-gemini-2-5-pro-to-ai-mode)). Apple's iOS 26 waits on hold for you ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/i-tried-hold-assist-in-ios-26-and-it-solves-one-of-the-most-annoying-things-about-phone-calls-heres-how-it-works)). Infinitus has made millions of AI calls to insurers **(self-reported)** ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/infinitus-systems-secures-515m-automating-healthcare-calls)). Every serious player **announces it is automated**.
- **The real barriers** are identity and authorization (HIPAA), recording consent (California all-party consent), reps refusing third parties, the lack of a provider billing API, the "practice of law" line, and trust. The TCPA AI-voice ruling matters less than people assume for calls **to** business lines.

## 1. Other billing domains where the same engine applies

The engine has four steps: (1) reconcile the bill against the payer's own records, (2) find the **provider's own** published rules, (3) make an evidence-backed call, (4) track promises and follow up. A domain fits well when all three of these are true: errors are common, the provider publishes binding rules or policies, and resolution still happens by phone.

|---|---|---|---|

Negotiation happens over email with account execs, so the voice agent adds little. |
| **Vendor disputes / healthcare B2B** | Providers calling insurers is the most mature AI-calling market. Infinitus: $102.9M raised, 1M-4M+ payer calls **(self-reported)** ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/infinitus-systems-secures-515m-automating-healthcare-calls)). | Infinitus, SuperDial (provider-side calls to payers). | **Proof the voice step works at scale** in healthcare IVRs and hold queues, but on the provider side. Remy does the mirror image, for the patient. |

**Takeaway:** the engine is strongest where (a) the counterparty publishes binding rules, (b) dollars per case are high, and (c) phone is still the resolution channel. Hospital bills meet all three. Insurance denials and multi-site utilities meet two. Telecom meets (c) only. B2B AP and freight meet (a) and (b), but the channel is documents, not voice.

## 3. Why this hasn't been built or done well already

| Barrier | Detail and sources |
|---|---|
| **Hold times and IVRs** | Average US hold is ~17 minutes, and 23% of callers have waited 30+ minutes ([TCN survey](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210727005281/en/TCN-Consumer-Survey-Finds-Americans-Overwhelmingly-Prefer-to-Interact-with-a-Live-Person-When-Dealing-with-Customer-Service-Reps)). Human-pickup detection is imperfect (iOS false positives above). This made human-staffed negotiation expensive. AI waiting costs only telephony minutes. |
| **Reps refusing an AI or third party** | No published hospital policy on AI callers turned up, but billing offices verify identity (DOB, account number) and many refuse non-authorized third parties. Google's business opt-out shows the counterparty can block AI calls. Pine's model presents the AI as an "authorized representative, like a family member." |
| **HIPAA authorization and identity** | 45 CFR 164.510(b) lets providers disclose payment-relevant info to a person the patient has identified as involved in payment, if the patient doesn't object. Otherwise they need a signed authorization or a personal representative under 164.502(g) ([HHS FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/disclosures-to-family-and-friends)). In practice an AI agent needs either the patient conferenced in to say "yes, talk to them," or a signed HIPAA authorization on file with the hospital. Remy is not a covered entity itself, but it handles PHI, so it needs strong security and is likely bound by FTC Health Breach Notification and state health-privacy laws. |
| **Call recording consent** | California Penal Code 632/632.7 requires **all-party consent**. Per *Smith v. LoanMe*, a party to the call can be liable too, with $5,000 per violation ([Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/california-supreme-court-clarifies-requirement-of-two-party-consent-to)). New CIPA suits target AI vendors that capture calls, e.g. *Cresta* (Jun 2025) and *In re Otter.AI* (N.D. Cal. 2025) ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260220-a-mofo-privacy-minute-q-a-wiretapping-theories-reach-ai-notetakers), [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/print/v2/content/41622/ai-call-monitoring-lawsuits-are-heating-up%3A-5-steps-your-business-can-take-to-minimize-risk.pdf)). |
| **FCC Feb 2024 AI-voice ruling (TCPA)** | Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 holds that AI-generated voices are "artificial" under the TCPA, so the existing consent rules for artificial or prerecorded voice calls apply ([Cooley](https://cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-02-15-fcc-ai-generated-robocalls-illegal-under-the-tcpa), [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FCC-Extends-Regulatory-Reach-Over-AI-Announces-TCPA-Restrictions-Cover-AI-Generated-Voices-in-Outbound-Calls)). **Does it apply to calls TO businesses?** The artificial-voice restriction in 227(b)(1)(B) covers **residential lines**. 227(b)(1)(A) covers wireless numbers and certain special lines. A call to a hospital's business billing line, made because the consumer asked for it and not as telemarketing, appears to fall largely outside these consent requirements. My reading: low risk for calls to call-center landlines; higher if the agent ever calls a rep's mobile or calls consumers. Not settled law, so get counsel. |
| **State AI disclosure laws** | Utah SB 149 (2024): disclose generative AI if asked, and up front for regulated occupations ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/04/Utah-AI-Laws-Require-Consumer-Facing-Disclosures-Starting-May-1)). California BOT Act (B&P 17941): unlawful to mislead about bot identity to drive a sale ([Daily Journal](https://dailyjournal.com/articles/379909-california-s-bolstering-online-transparency-act-targets-bot-disclosures-in-elections-and-transactions)). California AB 2905 (2024): auto-dialer prerecorded messages must say if the voice is AI ([Assembly analysis](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-04/ab-2905-low-apcp-analysis.pdf)). Always disclosing removes most of this risk. |
| **Unauthorized practice of law / debt relief** | *Upsolve v. James* (2d Cir., Sep 9, 2025): New York's UPL statutes **can** apply to nonlawyers giving debt-collection advice. The court vacated the injunction that had protected Upsolve ([Pro Bono Institute](https://www.probonoinst.org/2025/10/07/setback-for-justice-advocates-in-upsolve-litigation/)). FTC TSR debt-relief rule: for-profit services telemarketed to consumers can't take fees before settling or reducing unsecured debt ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/federal_register_notices/telemarketing-sales-rule-final-rule-amendments/100810tsr.pdf)). Applying it to medical-bill correction is unclear, but success-only fees comply anyway. DoNotPay's FTC order (above) shows the marketing risk. |
| **Liability for wrong advice** | If the agent asserts a wrong rule ("you must give me 80% off") or agrees to a payment plan, the patient is harmed and the hospital's trust in the AI drops. LLM hallucination of policy text is the core risk. |
| **Data access** | Hospitals have **no billing API** for patients. Itemized bills and CPT codes have to be requested (the Rosenberg case had to ask for them). Payer-side data is better: the CMS Patient Access API (CMS-9115-F) requires MA, Medicaid, CHIP, and FFE QHP plans to expose claims via FHIR, e.g. CARIN Blue Button ExplanationOfBenefit ([CMS FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/about-cms/obrhi/interoperability/faqs/patient-access-api)). Employer plans are not covered. |
| **Unit economics of % of savings** | Market rates are 30-60% of savings (advocates ~30%, Trim ~33%, Billshark 40%, Rocket 35-60%). Hospital outcomes are lumpy and slow, taking months of follow-up. Patients with charity-eligible income can't pay big fees, and charging them a large share of charity-care relief looks predatory. Google, Apple, and possibly Rocket Rowan are commoditizing the call itself. |
| **Trust** | Users must hand over bank, insurance, and ID data ([Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/07/10/online-bill-negotiators-promise-savings-require-personal-data-access/)). Billing reps distrust AI callers, especially after the Duplex controversy. Pine's Trustpilot complaints show how fragile trust is. |

### What changed recently to make this possible now

- **LLM tool use plus low-latency speech-to-speech.** OpenAI's Realtime API went GA with `gpt-realtime` on Aug 28, 2025, including **SIP phone calling** and remote MCP tools ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-realtime/), [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2025/09/openai-gpt-realtime)). Infinitus shows healthcare IVRs and hold queues are solved at scale.
- **Price transparency.** The CMS hospital price transparency rule has been in effect since **Jan 1, 2021**, and the payer Transparency-in-Coverage machine-readable files since **Jul 2022**. Enforcement is growing: 10 hospitals fined in 2025 vs. 3 in 2024, 27 in total ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/hospital-price-transparency-fine-enforcement-in-2025-7-things-to-know/)). Chargemasters and negotiated rates are now public evidence.
- **California AB 1020 (2021, eff. Jan 1, 2022).** Charity care and discount eligibility rose to **400% FPL**. "High medical costs" now means out-of-pocket costs above 10% of income. No credit reporting or lawsuits for 180 days. HCAI has enforced it since Jan 2024 ([HCAI fact sheet](https://hcai.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Health-Care-Debt-and-Fair-Billing-Program-Fact-Sheet-January-2022.pdf)). **AB 2297 (2024)** bars counting monetary assets for eligibility (eff. Jan 1, 2025) ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/california-hospitals-fair-pricing-act-changes)). These are exactly the "provider's own rules" Remy cites.
- **California SB 1061 (signed Sep 2024).** Bans most medical debt from credit reports. Most sources give Jan 1, 2025 as the effective date ([KEYT](https://keyt.com/news/california/2024/09/24/new-state-law-prevents-medical-debt-from-being-used-by-credit-reporting-agencies-effective-jan-1/), [CALPIRG](https://pirg.org/california/media-center/statement-governor-newsom-signs-bill-to-tackle-medical-debt/)). Check the date against leginfo.
- **CFPB medical debt rule.** Finalized Jan 2025 to remove ~$49B in medical debt from ~15M credit reports. **Vacated Jul 11, 2025** (E.D. Tex., Judge Jordan), with the administration joining industry. The opinion also said FCRA preempts state laws. NCLC disputes that the ruling has legal effect on the 15 states with their own bans ([Brownstein](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/federal-court-vacates-cfpbs-medical-debt-rule-finds-fcra-preempts-state-laws/), [NCLC](https://library.nclc.org/article/latest-keeping-medical-debt-out-credit-reports)). No appeal was found. Net effect: federal protection is gone, state protection is uncertain, and patients need correction **before** the debt goes to collections.
- **No Surprises Act PPDR.** Uninsured or self-pay patients billed **≥$400 over the Good Faith Estimate** can file a dispute within 120 days ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medical-bill-rights/help/dispute-a-bill)). This is another deterministic, rule-based trigger.
- **Consumer acceptance of AI callers.** Google ships AI business calling to all US users, Apple ships Hold Assist, and Pine raised $25M.

## 4. Limitations and mitigations (Devpost-ready)

| Limitation | Remy mitigation (has / could have) |
|---|---|
| Hold times / IVR | Agent waits on hold and navigates IVR at telephony cost. Verify a human picked up (keyword/turn detection) before speaking. Patient gets a "rep is on" ping. |
| Rep refuses to talk to an AI or third party | **Patient can be conferenced in** to give verbal permission. The agent states its purpose and offers to send the written dispute packet instead. Fallback: generate a letter or portal message for the patient to send. |
| HIPAA / identity | Signed HIPAA authorization (per hospital form) collected up front. Agent discloses only the minimum needed. The patient's own verification answers are relayed only with patient approval. PHI encrypted, never used for training. |
| Call recording consent (CA all-party) | **Announces up front that the call is automated and recorded** and asks for consent. If the rep declines, it continues without recording and keeps text notes only. |
| FCC 2024 TCPA AI-voice ruling | Calls only business billing lines, only at the patient's request, never telemarketing, never calls consumers. Logs who initiated and why. |
| State AI disclosure laws (Utah, CA BOT Act, AB 2905) | **Always discloses it is an automated assistant** acting for the named patient, at the start and whenever asked. Never imitates a human. |
| UPL / debt-relief rules | Cites only the provider's **own published policies** and public rules. No legal advice, no threats of litigation. Asks for correction, not "settlement." Success fee only, charged after the corrected bill posts (TSR-safe). |
| Liability for wrong claims / hallucination | **Deterministic math with cited sources.** Every claimed discount links to a policy page, chargemaster line, or statute. LLM output is checked against the evidence pack before the call. **Human-in-the-loop approval** of the script and of any commitment. |
| Agent agreeing to something harmful | **Never pays, never agrees to a payment plan, never shares card info.** Commitments are logged as "promises" for patient approval. |
| Promises not honored (Pine's "may not reflect on bill") | **Promise tracker.** Records rep name, reference number, and promised amount and date. Re-checks the bill and bank feed, then auto-follows up and escalates. |
| No hospital billing API | Patient uploads the itemized bill, or the agent requests the itemized bill and CPT codes on the first call. Payer EOBs via the CMS Patient Access API (FHIR) where available. Bank feed via the user's aggregator link. |
| Unit economics / fairness | Free tier for charity-care eligible patients (subsidized by others). Low success fee capped in dollars. Most of the work runs on machine time, not human negotiators. |
| Trust / transparency | **Full audit log**: transcript, evidence pack, and timeline visible to the patient. Read-only data access. Patient can stop, listen live, or take over the call. |
| Counterparty opt-out (as Google allows) | Honors a do-not-call-by-AI list per facility and switches to letter mode. |

## 5. Expansion story

Healthcare first, because that is where the rule-to-dollar gap is widest. Americans owe at least **$220 billion** in medical debt ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/issue-brief/the-burden-of-medical-debt-in-the-united-states/)), and about **45% of nonprofit hospital organizations bill patients who already qualify for their own charity care**, which hospitals estimate at **$2.7 billion** ([KHN via NPQ](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/study-finds-45-percent-of-patients-who-qualify-for-charity-care-are-billed-anyway/)). Hospital bills come with unusually explicit, enforceable provider rules: 501(r) financial-assistance policies, California's 400%-FPL fair-pricing law, public chargemasters, and the No Surprises Act's $400 GFE trigger. They are also still resolved by phone. Patients almost never push back: fewer than **1%** of denied ACA claims are ever appealed ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2023/)).

The same four-step engine (reconcile records, find the provider's own rule, call with evidence, track the promise) carries over directly. First come adjacent healthcare disputes such as insurer denials, using the plan document plus FHIR EOBs. Then consumer bills where the provider's terms are public and phone is the channel: **$164B/yr** of cable and internet ([Doxo](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/report-u-s-households-spend-164-billion-annually-on-cable-and-internet-services/)), outage credits, and fee reversals. B2B is last, where duplicate and erroneous payments run **0.8-2% of AP disbursements** ([APQC via CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/metric-of-the-month-detect-and-prevent-duplicate-or-erroneous-payments/656852/)) and 3-6% of freight invoices carry errors ([Intellitrans](https://www.intellitrans.com/resources/what-freight-audit-catches-rail-billing-errors)). In each new vertical, only the rulebook connector and the evidence templates change. The reconciliation math, the disclosure-first voice agent, and the promise tracker stay the same.

## Source caveats

- Hospital bill error-rate claims (the widely repeated "80%") are poorly sourced. Estimates range from ~7% to ~90% depending on definition ([roundup](https://etactics.com/blog/medical-billing-error-statistics)). Don't put "80% of bills have errors" in the pitch. Use the charity-care and KFF figures instead.
- Pine, Claimable, Tropic, PRGX, and Infinitus numbers are self-reported.
- The SaaS unused-license share (46-53%) is secondary; only the $4,830/employee figure is from Zylo's own release.
- The SB 1061 effective date conflicts across sources (Jan 1 vs. Jul 1, 2025).
- The TCPA reading for calls to business lines is my analysis of 227(b)(1)(B)'s residential-line scope, not a confirmed ruling.
