# Remy: Competitive Landscape and Market Facts (healthcare billing)

Research date: 2026-10-25. Web search/fetch only, no paid APIs. Most traction and savings figures are **company-reported and unaudited**; this is flagged where it applies. "Not found" means I searched and could not find a public statement. It does not mean the company definitely lacks the feature.

---

## 1. Competitors and close alternatives

### A. Human or hybrid medical-bill advocates (patient-paid, contingency)

**Goodbill** (goodbill.com)
- **What it does:** The patient authorizes Goodbill to pull hospital billing records. Its "software and experts" look for coding errors and pricing discrepancies, then negotiate. It uses federal hospital price-transparency data. ([FAQ](https://www.goodbill.com/faq))
- **Who pays and how much:** The patient pays 20% of savings, capped at $1,000, and nothing if there are no savings. ([help center](https://help.goodbill.com/en/articles/254209))
- **Scope limits:** Hospital bills only "for now." No bills in collections and no bills already paid. ([FAQ](https://www.goodbill.com/faq))
- **Charity care:** It mentions financial-aid programs for uninsured patients. A competitor's comparison page says it does 501(r) screening. ([CareRoute comparison](https://www.careroute.ai/bill-defense/compare-medical-bill-negotiation-services), a competitor-authored page)
- **Bank data:** No. **Calls:** humans negotiate; no AI calling found.
- **Claims:** 20–60% average savings, up to 95%. Self-reported, with no stated method.
- **Weaknesses:** Hospital-only and unpaid-only, so it cannot recover money you already paid or find duplicate payments. The analysis is a black box and the negotiation is human.

**Resolve Medical Bills** (resolvemedicalbills.com)
- **What it does:** Pairs the patient with a dedicated human advocate who builds a savings plan, negotiates, appeals denials and helps with financial aid. ([site](https://www.resolvemedicalbills.com/))
- **Who pays and how much:** The patient pays a deposit plus a success fee. Bills of $5K–15K: $250 deposit + 25%. Bills of $15K–200K: $499 + 10%. Over $200K: $499 + 10% or less. Minimum fee is $1,000 ($2,500 for bills over $200K). The deposit is refunded if there are no savings.
- **Funding:** $3.3M seed led by AlleyCorp, July 2022. ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220718005136/en/Resolve-Raises-3.3M-in-Seed-Funding))
- **Traction:** Site says "$55.2M total savings to date." Self-reported.
- **Bank data:** No. **Calls:** human advocates.
- **Weaknesses:** Effectively only bills of $5K or more, an upfront deposit, a $1,000 minimum fee, and a human-labor bottleneck.

**CareRoute "Bill Defense"** (careroute.ai, Healtheja Inc.)
- **What it does:** A patient app plus a human team. It reviews errors, applies financial assistance, disputes insurer issues and negotiates with providers. It covers hospital, physician, lab, imaging and collections bills. ([site](https://www.careroute.ai/))
- **Charity care:** It publishes per-hospital financial-assistance guides, including California ones such as Cedars-Sinai, so it overlaps directly with our California charity-care angle. ([Cedars guide](https://www.careroute.ai/hospital-financial-assistance/cedars-sinai))
- **Who pays and how much:** 18–25% of savings, capped at $1,000. There is also an app subscription. ([comparison](https://www.careroute.ai/bill-defense/compare-medical-bill-negotiation-services))
- **Claims:** A reduction on 64% of cases, about $1,500 average saved, 2k+ users. Self-reported.
- **Bank data / AI calls:** Not found.
- **Weaknesses:** Small, human-team bottleneck, and it repeats the unsourced "80% of bills have errors" claim.

**CoPatient** (Boston, since about 2013)
- **What it does:** Free review, then 35% of savings found. Raised $3.6M. Claims errors on 80% of bills reviewed. Self-reported, and the sources date from 2014–15, so it may be inactive or changed. ([Becker's](https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-coding-billing-and-collections/copatient-co-fights-billing-discrepancies-with-consumers.html), [406 Ventures](https://www.406ventures.com/news/1538-medical-bill-savings-startup-copatient-moves-back-to-boston-after-raising-3-6m))

**Medical Cost Advocate**
- **What it does:** Human bill negotiators. Claims 20–50% savings. It has merged into the benefit-navigation company Emry Health, and its fee is not published. ([key.com hub](https://www.key.com/to/fwhub+medicalcostadvocate))
- **Industry benchmark:** KFF Health News puts typical advocate fees at about 30% of savings, with a range of 15–50%. ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/advocate-sidebar))

**Truffle Health** (New York, founded 2022)
- **What it does:** Software that detects billing errors, audits charges, analyzes the patient's plan and educates on patient rights. Pricing not found. ([Cornell Tech](https://tech.cornell.edu/built/truffle-health), [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/truffle-health))

**Sheer Health**
- **What it does:** A consumer app plus human team, about 3 years old as of late 2025. The patient links an insurance account and uploads bills and claims. It gives free AI + human answers. Patients pay for denied-claim appeals and out-of-network reimbursement, either through membership tiers or a percentage of money recovered. It handles communication with insurers. ([Stateline, Nov 2025](https://stateline.org/2025/11/20/patients-deploy-bots-to-battle-health-insurers-that-deny-care/), [Cleveland Moms](https://theclevelandmoms.com/sheer-health-a-new-app-that-saves-moms-time-money/))
- **Funding:** Founder Collective portfolio; amount not found. ([Founder Collective](https://foundercollective.com/portfolio/sheer-health/))
- **Traction:** "$5M returned to members" as of October 2024. Self-reported.
- **Weaknesses:** Insurer and claims oriented rather than hospital charity care. AI voice calling not found.

### B. Free nonprofits

**Dollar For** (dollarfor.org)
- **What it does:** The closest mission match. It screens the patient for nonprofit-hospital charity care. The patient uploads tax returns, pay stubs and **bank statements** (used as income proof, not reconciliation). Dollar For prepares the application in 7–10 days and follows up by email and text. It uses an AI tool, but "all work done by AI is reviewed by a real Patient Advocate." ([FAQ](https://dollarfor.org/faq/))
- **Important limit:** Hospitals typically won't speak to Dollar For directly; the patient relays updates.
- **Who pays:** Free; funded by donors and foundations. It is an OpenAI Foundation People-First AI Fund grantee.
- **Traction:** More than $55M erased in 2025, despite losing about a third of its budget. ([KFF Health News podcast, Jan 2026](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/)) One June 2026 report cites 50,000 applications and $151M+ total; another profile cites $157M. Both are secondary sources.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Charity care only.
  - Paper and email applications only; no calling.
  - No reconciliation of bill vs EOB vs payments.
  - No error, duplicate or price-file checking.
  - Capacity limited by advocates.

**Undue Medical Debt** (formerly RIP Medical Debt)
- **What it does:** Buys medical debt in bulk and abolishes it. Patients cannot apply; relief depends on which debts it acquires. Eligibility is 400% FPL or below, or medical debt of 5% of income or more.
- **Traction:** $20.3B abolished for 13M people as of June 2025, per its own release. ([press](https://unduemedicaldebt.org/press-release/undue-medical-debt-announces-20-billion-in-medical-debt-erased-reaching-historic-milestone/)) In April 2025 it bought the Pendrick portfolio: $30B in debt, about 20M people.
- **Not a direct competitor:** It is complementary and does not act on a specific patient's current bill.

**Counterforce Health** (Raleigh / Durham, NC)
- **What it does:** A free AI tool that writes insurance denial appeals, with an optional case manager. ([Axios Raleigh, Aug 2025](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce))
- **Funding:** Grants, with conflicting totals: $280K, or a $2.47M PennAITech grant.
- **Traction:** About 20,000 people helped, with a 70–75% success claim. Self-reported.
- **Weaknesses:** Covers denials only. Not hospital bills, charity care or calls.

### C. AI-first patient tools (2024–2026)

**Claimable** (Sacramento; founder Warris Bokhari MD)
- **What it does:** AI insurance-denial appeal letters for about $40–50 per appeal.
- **Traction:** Claims an 80% success rate and $30M+ recovered (TIME100 Health, Feb 2026). Seed from Walkabout Ventures and Humanrace Capital; amount undisclosed. Now selling to drugmakers and health systems. ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/revenue-cycle-management/a-third-player-enters-the-ai-arms-race/), [TIME](https://tollbit.time.com/collections/time100-health-2026/7362601/warris-bokhari/))
- **Weaknesses:** Denials only. No hospital bill or charity care work, no calls.

**Avelis Health** (YC; $500K pre-seed, June 2025)
- **What it did:** AI agents that audit and negotiate patient bills and contact hospitals and insurers by **phone, email and fax**. Claims about 60 patients and $300K+ reduced in 5 months. Self-reported. ([AfroTech](https://afrotech.com/avelis-health-secures-500k-boost), [founded.com](https://www.founded.com/this-founder-spent-a-decade-fighting-250k-in-inaccurate-medical-bills-now-hes-using-ai-to-fight-yours/))
- **Pivot:** It has since moved to **payment integrity for self-insured employers and health plans**, using voice agents to request records and recover overpayments. ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OEB-avelis-health-preventing-overpayments-on-medical-claims))
- **Why it matters:** This is the clearest proof that the "AI calls the hospital for the patient" idea was tried and then moved to B2B. Consumer unit economics are the risk.

**Pine AI** (19Pine; $25M Series A led by Fortwest, Dec 2025)
- **What it does:** A general consumer agent that **places phone calls** and sends emails to negotiate bills, cancel subscriptions and file complaints. It explicitly markets hospital and medical bill negotiation. It has launched Pine Voice with its own voice model (May 2026). ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2025/12/03/pine-launches-ai-agent-as-it-secures-25m-series-a/), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/06/pine-ai-aims-consumer-ai-agent-complex-customer-service-interactions/), [Pine medical page](https://www.19pine.ai/es/hospital-and-medical-negotiation))
- **Pricing:** A directory lists $150/month and freemium; unverified.
- **Traction:** A 93% negotiation success rate and $3M+ saved, mostly telecom (AT&T, Charter, Verizon). Self-reported.
- **Why it matters:** **This is the most direct competitor on AI calling.**
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Horizontal product with no healthcare domain depth.
  - No EOB reconciliation, no charity-care law engine, no price files.
  - Its medical track record is undocumented.

**Other general consumer calling agents** (Instinct, Genspark, Tab, Szn and others)
- Venture funding for personal agents reached $1.8B so far in 2026. ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/charts/personal-ai-agent-startups))
- None of them is healthcare-specific, but each could add a "call your hospital" use case.

**Self-serve AI bill checkers**
- **Products:**
  - MyMedBill: $9.99/month, scripts and letters, financial-aid screening, no calls, pre-launch. ([site](https://mymedbill.co/))
  - VerifyDoc: $19 flat.
  - IsMyBillWrong.
  - MedBillAI: runs on Claude.
  - MD Bill Check.
  - Bill Sherlock: launched June 2026, compares charges to Medicare rates plus 5,000+ facility prices, freemium. ([PeerPush](https://peerpush.com/p/bill-sherlock))
- **Substitute:** ChatGPT prompts that compare a bill with its EOB. ([Mind Over Money](https://www.mindovermoney.ai/prompt-library/ai-prompt-to-check-medical-bill/))
- **Weaknesses:**
  - The patient still makes every call.
  - None found uses bank data.
  - Error claims are unsourced, and there is no independent accuracy testing.
  - They are LLM-generated, so figures may be hallucinated.

**Older and failed attempts**
- DisputeBills: 2016, $400K, 30% of savings. ([Vator](https://vator.tv/news/2016-02-03-disputebills-raises-400k-to-lower-your-medical-bills))
- Remedy: $1.9M; shut down, with the founder blaming restricted access to patient data. ([Coverager](https://coverager.com/the-end-of-the-road-for-two-healthcare-startups/))

### D. Employer and plan navigators (B2B2C)

**Transcarent / Accolade**
- Transcarent completed its $621M acquisition of Accolade in 2025. Claims and benefits specialists resolve claim disputes for employer members. Reaches about 20M people. ([The Middle Market](https://www.themiddlemarket.com/latest-news/transcarent-completes-621m-accolade-acquisition))
- **Weakness:** Only reachable if your employer buys it.

**Collective Health**
- Member Advocates provide claims and benefits help for self-funded employer plans. ([Providence/Collective page](https://cd104.providencehealthplan.com/public-employees-benefit-board-pebb/benefit-resources))
- **Weakness:** Plan-side, so it does not fight the plan's own adjudication or work on hospital charity care.

**Health Advocate**
- About $50–100 per case or around 25% of savings for consumers; mostly sold to employers. ([NBC Bay Area](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/health/the-bill-fighter-how-private-patient-advocates-battle-big-hospital-bills/2243740/?amp=1))

**Baba** (Medicare)
- $6.5M seed (Feb 2026) and a $40M Series A led by FirstMark with CVS Health Ventures (Oct 20, 2026).
- AI plus human Medicare navigation and advocacy, sold to health plans. 10K+ patients served. ([Fierce Healthcare](https://fiercehealthcare.com/finance/baba-nabs-40m-series-backed-firstmark-capital-and-cvs-health-ventures-medicare-ai-care))
- **Why it matters:** Shows investor appetite for AI patient advocates, but the channel is payer-paid.

### E. Hospital / provider-side (they answer or place the call on the other end)

**Cedar** (patient billing platform)
- **Kora voice agent:** Launched April 2025 and built with Twilio. The Kora Platform (Aug 2026) adds autonomous inbound and outbound voice plus two-way text.
- **Reported results:** 600K+ inbound conversations, 40% containment, 7.4% more payment attempts than live agents. ApolloMD reported a 42% increase in collections. ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/news/366649202/Cedar-deploys-agentic-AI-for-patient-billing-financial-experience))
- **Affordability features:** Affordability Navigator and an "80 attributes" engine route at-risk patients to financial-assistance applications and Medicaid enrollment. ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/medical-billing-startup-cedar-roll-out-new-features-connect-patient-financial), [HIT Consultant Apr 2026](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/04/08/cedar-intelligence-ai-expansion-patient-financial-experience-billing/))
- **Why it matters:** Cedar is paid by providers and optimized for collections. Its CSO markets an "agentic advocate in every patient's pocket," which directly contests our framing. Remy's agent will often be talking to a Kora-type bot.

**Waystar** (RCM, public company)
- Presumptive charity screening, from its 2019 PARO acquisition, scores hospital accounts against the hospital's financial assistance policy using public-record data, without credit checks. PARO screened about 11M visits in 2018 and flagged 3.5M as eligible (vendor figure). ([Waystar](https://waystar.com/our-platform/financial-clearance/charity-screening/), [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/?p=49184))
- **Why it matters:** Hospitals already *can* auto-detect charity eligibility. AB 1312 will make this mandatory in California from July 2027 (see section 4).

**AI voice for provider billing teams**
- Hello Patient: $22.5M Series A, Sept 2025, $100M valuation; billing calls for Piedmont Urgent Care. ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/hello-patient-secures-225m-investors-bet-ai-voice-agent-growth), [Hypepotamus](https://www.hypepotamus.com/georgia-urgent-care-ai-patient-billing-calls/))
- LunaBill: YC F25, insurer claim-status calls, 60K+ calls. ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Ooq-lunabill-ai-voice-callers-for-healthcare-billing-teams))
- Prosper AI: $30M Series A led by a16z.
- Sunlight AI: $17.4M seed, payer underpayment arbitration.
- Toothy.ai: dental insurance calls.

**Price-data vendors**
- Turquoise Health: $40M Series C, March 2026; price-transparency data moving into contracts and payments; B2B. ([BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20260317136906/en/Turquoise-Health-Announces-%2440-Million-Series-C-to-Become-the-Operating-System-for-Healthcare-Contracts-and-Payments))
- **Why it matters:** Possible data source, or a future competitor if it moves to consumers.

## 3. Analysis

### Remy's genuine differentiators
1. **Three-way reconciliation using the patient's bank transactions.** I found no consumer tool that matches bill + EOB + actual payments. Plaid appears in medical *payment* apps such as PayGround, not in auditing. That reconciliation is what surfaces "payment the hospital never credited" and duplicate payments. Goodbill explicitly refuses already-paid bills, so this is open ground.
2. **A charity-care entitlement computed from the hospital's own policy plus California statute** (400% FPL floor, no asset test), turned into a cited legal ask. Dollar For does the application on paper. Remy turns it into a demand on the phone, alongside error and duplicate disputes in the same call.
3. **The patient's AI agent actually calls the billing office.**
   - Human advocates: Goodbill, Resolve, CareRoute.
   - Letters only: Dollar For, Claimable, Counterforce, MyMedBill.
   - The only AI callers found are Pine, which is horizontal, and Avelis, which pivoted to B2B.
4. **Case management after the call.** Remy captures the case number, keeps a ledger of promises, scores what the call won and plans the next call. Competitors either hand this to a human or leave it to the patient. Dollar For has the patient relay hospital updates.
5. **Every figure is deterministic and cited.** This contrasts with LLM bill checkers and with unsourced "80% of bills have errors" marketing. It matters because DoNotPay paid $193K to the FTC in 2025 over unsubstantiated AI "robot lawyer" claims. ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87474))
6. **Transparent guardrails.** The agent says it is automated, has written authorization and never agrees to pay. California's bot-disclosure law and the FCC's 2024 TCPA ruling on AI voices make disclosure a feature, not a cost. ([FCC ruling summary](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/fcc-issues-declaratory-ruling-on-ai-generated-voice-robocalls)) Note that the TCPA mainly governs calls *to* consumers. Calls to a business billing line are lower risk, but recording calls in California still needs every party's consent (Penal Code 632).

### Where competitors are clearly ahead (honest)
- **Track record and trust:**
  - Dollar For: more than $55M erased in 2025 and tens of thousands of applications.
  - Resolve: $55M claimed.
  - Undue: $20B+.
  - Sheer: $5M returned.
  - Remy has zero outcomes.
- **Humans for hard cases:** human negotiators handle escalation, supervisors and judgment calls on large bills. Resolve targets bills of $5K–200K, where a human's 10% fee is worth paying.
- **Insurer appeals:** Claimable (80%, $30M+) and Counterforce do denial appeals well. Remy's scope is the hospital billing office, not insurer appeals.
- **Calling infrastructure:** Pine has $25M, its own voice model and real call volume. Cedar has 600K+ conversations. They will out-execute us on raw voice quality.
- **Distribution:** employer navigators (Accolade/Transcarent, Collective) and payer-paid Baba reach patients without them having to buy anything.
- **The hospital side is automating too.** Hospitals that use Waystar or Cedar may already screen for charity care. California AB 1312 (presumptive screening from July 1, 2027) will mandate some of what we do. The value shifts to verifying that the hospital actually applied the discount.
- **Hospitals refuse to talk to third parties.** Dollar For reports that hospitals usually won't speak with it directly. Remy's authorization flow and HIPAA/agent identity are unproven against that wall.
- **Unit economics:** Avelis tried consumer AI calling and moved to B2B within months. Contingency fees on small bills are thin.

### "Why we're different" (one paragraph)
> Medical-bill help today is either a human advocate who takes 20–35% of what they save, or a free nonprofit that files a paper charity-care application and waits. AI tools so far mostly write letters and leave the phone call to you. Remy is the first patient-side agent we know of that does the whole loop. It checks the hospital bill against your insurance EOB and your own bank transactions to catch payments the hospital never credited, adjustments it never applied and duplicate charges. It reads that hospital's own financial-assistance policy, plus California's 400%-of-poverty charity-care law and the hospital's published prices, to compute the discount you are legally owed. Then it makes the call. Our voice agent identifies itself as automated, acts under your written authorization and never agrees to pay. It walks the billing office through each ask, gets a case number, logs every promise and plans the follow-up call when a promise slips. Every dollar figure comes from deterministic code with a cited source, the bill line, the EOB line, the bank transaction or the policy clause, so the AI can argue but never invent a number.

(Use "the first we know of" deliberately. Pine AI and the 2025 version of Avelis place AI calls, so do not claim "the only AI that calls hospitals.")

## 4. Market facts for the impact section (with dates and evidence quality)

### Medical debt totals
- **KFF / Peterson (Feb 12, 2024; data from the 2021 SIPP survey):** at least **$220B** owed. **20M adults (about 1 in 12) owe more than $250**, 14M owe more than $1,000 and 3M owe more than $10,000. **Strong evidence** (federal survey), but the data are from 2021. ([Health System Tracker](https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/the-burden-of-medical-debt-in-the-united-states/))
- **KFF Health Care Debt Survey (2022):** **41% of adults** have some health-care debt, under a broader definition that includes credit cards and family loans. ([same source](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/the-burden-of-medical-debt-in-the-united-states/))
- **Urban Institute (published Oct 2026; Dec 2025 survey):**
  - **27% of adults** say they or family owed medical debt in 2025, including 26% of insured adults.
  - 35% of those with debt were contacted by collectors.
  - 54% owe $1,000 or more and 16% owe $5,000 or more.
  - Collections on credit reports fell from 19.5% (2013) to 5% (2023) after credit-bureau policy changes.
  - Retrieved via secondary coverage. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/09/quarter-americans-medical-debt), [Chief Healthcare Executive](https://www.chiefhealthcareexecutive.com/view/medical-debt-in-america-7-key-takeaways)) Cite Urban's report directly once located.
- **CFPB:**
  - The final rule (January 2025) would have removed about **$49–50B** from the credit reports of about **15M** people. A federal judge vacated it on **July 11, 2025** (*Cornerstone Credit Union League v. CFPB*, E.D. Tex.). ([CFPB archive](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-finalizes-rule-to-remove-medical-bills-from-credit-reports/), [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/07/15/federal-judge-vacates-cfpb-medical-debt-rule/))
  - CFPB's March 2022 report estimated **$88B** of medical debt on credit reports as of June 2021. That is from memory and was not re-fetched in this session, so verify before citing.
  - This makes state law (California's SB 1061) the operative protection.
- **Uninsured trend:** Cedar says about 40% of collectible dollars on its platform now come from uninsured patients, up 54% in 3 years, and expects the One Big Beautiful Bill Act to push that higher. This is a vendor figure. ([TechTarget, Aug 2026](https://www.techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/news/366649202/Cedar-deploys-agentic-AI-for-patient-billing-financial-experience))

### Share of bills with errors (evidence is weak; handle carefully)
- **"80% of medical bills contain errors": WEAK / industry-sourced.** It traces to Medical Billing Advocates of America and to advocates paid by results. There is no published method, and MBAA's own CEO has said "3 out of 4." ([Consumerist 2009](https://consumerist.com/2009/05/14/80-of-medical-bills-have-errors/), [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/medical-billing-errors-growing-says-medical-billing-advocates-of-america/), [Incidental Economist critique](https://theincidentaleconomist.com/wordpress/medical-billing-errors/)) **Do not use it in public copy.** CareRoute, MedBillAI and CoPatient all repeat it.
- **Better alternatives:**
  - **KFF Health Care Debt Survey (2022):** **43% of all adults**, and 53% of those with health-care debt, say they received a medical or dental bill they *thought* contained an error. About two-thirds of those errors were billing for something insurance should have covered. This is a **perception-based but rigorous survey**. ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/the-burden-of-medical-debt-in-the-united-states/))
  - **KFF Consumer Experiences with Health Insurance survey (Feb–Mar 2023, n=3,605):** 58% of insured adults had a problem using their coverage, and about 30% say insurance paid less than expected. ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2023/06/survey-58-of-americans-had-at-least-1-issue-with-health-coverage-in-the-last-year/))
  - **AMA (2013):** about **7.1% of paid claims** had an error. This is an older, claim-level, industry figure; NerdWallet's 2014 estimate of 49% of Medicare claims is similarly weak. ([Incidental Economist](https://theincidentaleconomist.com/wordpress/medical-billing-errors/))
- **Suggested phrasing:** "4 in 10 U.S. adults say they've received a medical bill they believe contained an error (KFF, 2022)."

### People who qualify for charity care but don't get it
- **KFF (Nov 2022):** it is "unclear" what share of eligible patients receive charity care. Hospitals reported **$28B** in charity care in fiscal 2019. Half of hospitals spend **1.4% or less** of operating expenses on it. Nonprofits self-estimated **$2.7B** of 2019 bad debt as coming from patients likely eligible for charity care; this is unaudited and covers nonprofits only. ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/hospital-charity-care-how-it-works-and-why-it-matters/))
- **KFF Health News / Washington Post (Oct 2019):** **45% of nonprofit hospital organizations** routinely bill patients whose incomes qualify them for charity care. Based on IRS Schedule H filings. ([Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/study-finds-45-percent-of-patients-who-qualify-for-charity-care-are-billed-anyway/))
- **Dollar For, "Bridging the Chasm" (Apr 2024):** **at least $14B a year** is billed that should be waived as charity care.
  - Method: $2.7B self-reported, extrapolated to all nonprofit and for-profit hospitals (about $7B), then doubled because a Maryland study of matched income records found hospitals self-report less than half.
  - **Advocacy estimate with a transparent but assumption-heavy method.** It includes the Providence settlement: about $21M refunded and $137M forgiven (Washington AG). (Dollar For PDF, dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)
- **North Carolina State Treasurer + NASHP (Jan 2022):** 18 of 105 nonprofit hospitals billed **$149.2M** to patients who should have qualified under the hospitals' own policies. ([NC Newsline](https://ncnewsline.com/2022/01/27/nc-nonprofit-hospitals-under-scrutiny-for-billing-millions-to-poor-patients-in-lieu-of-providing-charity-care/))
- **Lown Institute Fair Share report (Apr 2025; data 2020–22; about 1,800 nonprofit hospitals in 20 states):** **54%** have a "fair share deficit" (tax breaks exceed community investment), worth **$11.5B a year**. The prior report said 80% and $25.7B. Hospitals dispute the method. ([Lown PDF](https://lownhospitalsindex.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/fair-share-2025-national-report-20250409.pdf))
- **Health Affairs (2024):** nonprofit hospitals use "widely varying criteria" for free and discounted care. ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2023.01615))
- **NPR (May 2026):** average charity care is about 2.4% of operating budgets (Johns Hopkins / Zare). ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/11/nx-s1-5813781/charity-care-hospitals-medical-bills-uninsured))
- **Los Angeles County:** its hospitals reported $426.5M in financial assistance in 2023 (county report, Sept 2025). ([LA County](https://lacounty.gov/2025/09/16/los-angeles-county-releases-landmark-report-on-hospital-charity-care-and-launches-new-tools-to-expand-access-to-financial-assistance))
- **Unverified (do not use):** "Only 29% receive the assistance they qualify for; 52% never informed" (medicalbillfighter.org; no primary source found).

### California rules (primary source: [HCAI Hospital Fair Billing Program, laws page](https://hcai.ca.gov/affordability/hospital-fair-billing-program/laws-and-regulations))
- **AB 774 (2006, effective 2007):** the original Hospital Fair Pricing Act. Hospitals must have charity care and discount policies, and HCAI publishes them.
- **AB 1020 (Ch. 473, 2021):**
  - Raised the financial-assistance income floor from **350% to 400% FPL**.
  - Bars selling patient debt unless the patient was found ineligible or has not responded for 180 days.
  - Extended the wait before adverse credit reporting or a lawsuit from **150 to 180 days**.
  - Collectors must certify the patient was screened for coverage and financial assistance before suing.
  - HCAI enforcement began **Jan 1, 2024** (regulations: 22 CCR §96051 et seq.).
- **SB 1061 (Ch. 520, 2024) + AB 2297 (Ch. 511, 2024), effective Jan 1, 2025:**
  - **Bans adverse credit reporting** of hospital and medical debt.
  - **Removes monetary assets from eligibility**.
  - Defines "charity care" and "discount payment."
  - **Bans application deadlines**.
  - Bans requiring patients to apply for other coverage first.
  - Bans liens or sale of the patient's home.
  - Allows alternative income documentation.
  - HCAI authority now reaches back to violations from Jan 1, 2022.
  - One outlet (Spectrum News) gives July 1, 2025 for part of SB 1061, likely the contract provisions; confirm in the chaptered text. Medical credit cards and loans are *not* covered. ([KEYT](https://keyt.com/news/california/2024/09/24/new-state-law-prevents-medical-debt-from-being-used-by-credit-reporting-agencies-effective-jan-1/), [Spectrum](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/ca/california/health/2024/10/02/a-new-state-law-will-prohibit-medical-debt-from-appearing-on-credit-reports))
- **AB 1312 (Ch. 450, 2025; signed Oct 7, 2025):** starting **July 1, 2027**, hospitals must screen patients for financial assistance and **presume eligibility** for people enrolled in CalFresh, CalWORKs, WIC, Tribal TANF and similar programs. The screening group includes uninsured patients, Medi-Cal patients and those owing more than $500. The California Hospital Association opposed it unless amended. ([HCAI PIL 25-01](https://hcai.ca.gov/document/pil-25-01), [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/health/2025/07/medical-debt-relief-hospitals/))
  - **Strategic note:** this is a risk, because hospitals will do more screening themselves, and also a pitch line: "Remy checks that the hospital actually did what AB 1312 now requires."
- **Federal price transparency:** PatientRightsAdvocate found **49% of 2,000 hospitals fully compliant** (Sept 2026), up from 21% (Nov 2024). So price files are often missing or unusable, and Remy must handle that. ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/hospital-compliance-price-transparency-regulations-pra-report/830087/))
